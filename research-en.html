Research Directions

Research

Along the thread of interfaces, materials, and devices, we study all-solid-state batteries and high-energy cathodes, using synchrotron radiation, solid-state NMR, and Cs-corrected microscopy to see the reaction process clearly.

Research Framework

Three Threads

All three directions share one characterization and theory platform: materials design proposes hypotheses, advanced characterization provides evidence, and device validation decides what survives.

01

All-Solid-State Batteries

Across cathode materials and interfaces, solid electrolytes, and anode materials and interfaces, we study ion transport, space-charge layers, and coupled chemomechanical failure to build solid-state batteries that cycle stably.

  • Cathode / electrolyte interfaces
  • Solid electrolytes
  • Lithium-metal anodes
02

High-Energy Cathodes

For lithium-, sodium-, and magnesium-ion batteries, we study layered oxides, cobalt-free and low-cobalt cathodes, and multivalent-ion hosts, searching for a new balance among energy density, cycle life, and cost.

  • Layered oxides
  • Cobalt-free cathodes
  • Na / Mg-ion batteries
03

Advanced Characterization & Theory

Using synchrotron X-ray absorption and scattering, solid-state NMR, and Cs-corrected microscopy, combined with first-principles and molecular dynamics, we track how lattices, interfaces, and ion transport evolve under operating conditions.

  • Synchrotron
  • Solid-state NMR
  • Cs-corrected STEM / theory
Partner Platforms

The lab works with synchrotron sources, microscopy centers, and computing platforms, and supports joint training, short research visits, and shared beamtime. Bring us a concrete problem—we are happy to talk.
